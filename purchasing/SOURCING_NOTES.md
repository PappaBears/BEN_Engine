# BEN Engine — Sourcing notes

Prepared 2026-11-07 by the purchasing/sourcing pass. Companion files: `parts_catalog.json` (113 parts, the single source of
truth), `bom-<tier>.csv` + `docs/BOM-<tier>.md` (generated by `python -m ben_engine.bom --all`), `docs/BOM.md` (summary),
`EMAIL_QUEUE.md` + `email_queue/` (15 supplier questions, queued to Ben, nothing sent).

## 1. Method

1. The configuration model (`software/ben_engine/config.py`) decides quantities. Every catalog line has a `qty_rule` evaluated by a
   whitelisted expression evaluator (no `eval`) with variables `racks, it_kw, cooling_units_total, ups_modules, ups_kva_each,
   battery_kwh, service_breaker_a, total_kw, total_kva, nodes (= ceil(racks/2)+1), tier_len_m, generator (0/1)`.
   Lines whose quantity evaluates to 0 (e.g. generator items when `generator=False`) drop out.
2. Lines belong to a subsystem by code prefix (PWR power, CLG cooling, CMP compute, CTL controls). STR/ENV/FS/SITE/SVC always ride
   along when any subsystem is selected, because the enclosure, fire/safety, site and labour are needed for any delivered unit.
   Cooling lines additionally carry `applies_to_cooling` so DX, RDHx and DLC parts never appear together.
3. Pricing was researched by web search for distributor/reseller listings on 2026-11-07. **The build environment could not open
   vendor pages directly** (adafruit.com, digikey.com, cdw.com, olimex.com, reach.dog, containerone.net and others are outside the
   session's network allowlist), so every "confirmed" price was read from the vendor's own listing as surfaced in a search-result
   snippet (title + URL + price), not from a rendered page. Each confirmed line names the vendor, the URL, the price seen and
   competing listings. Treat them as "seen on 2026-11-07, re-verify before PO" — which is what the email queue does.

## 4. Price volatility, tariff and lead-time risks

- **DRAM/compute:** Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB MSRP rose $80 → $95 → ~$130 in 2026 (DRAM shortage); distributor prices $130–175. GPU
  servers and L40S "price hikes continue into Q1 2026"; H100 systems $236k–390k depending on allocation. Budget ±25 %.
- **Steel/containers:** one-trip container prices moved $5,450 → $4,850 within the Cascade listing's history; 40 ft HC backordered at
  ContainerOne on 2026-11-07. Section 232 steel/aluminium tariffs and any China-origin container duty change hit this line directly.
- **Copper:** feeders, transformers, busbars and PDU cords are copper-priced; the $18/A feeder estimate assumes 2026 Cu levels.
- **Lithium batteries:** Li-ion UPS cabinets ($55k each) are the second-largest non-compute cost; cell supply and UL 9540A
  documentation drive both price and AHJ approval. Tariffs on cells/BMS of Chinese origin are a live risk.
- **Cooling lead times:** CDUs 16–24 wk, in-row DX 8–12 wk, dry coolers 12–18 wk, RDHx 10–16 wk — the AI build-out has stretched all
  of them. Utility service interconnect (6–20 wk) is the site critical path and is outside our control.
- **Refrigerant transition:** ACRD301S is R410A; AIM Act phase-down may force R32/R454B variants — asked in email 04.
- **Low-stock items seen:** Rittal SK 3189.940 (1–2 pcs at distributors), Olimex -IND variant (12 wk), Fibox CAB PC 203018 (3 pcs),
  Eaton 93PM several SKUs at 0, Tripp Lite containment kits widely out of stock.

## 5. Make vs buy

| Buy | Why |
|---|---|
| ISO container (one-trip) | $4.7–4.9k for a CSC-certified, weather-tight, craneable steel shell is unbeatable; fabricating one is 5–10× the cost and loses the CSC plate. |
| Server racks (APC/Vertiv) | Certified 1,360–1,590 kg load, seismic kits, standard accessories; customers expect a known brand; $2.3–4k each. |
| Cooling (in-row DX, RDHx, CDU, dry cooler, cabinet AC) | Refrigeration/pressure equipment needs UL 60335-2-40 / UL 1995 listing, factory charge and warranty; not a startup core competence. |
| UPS + batteries | UL 1778 / UL 9540A listing and AHJ acceptance are only practical with OEM products. |
| Fire suppression | Must be designed and installed by a licensed fire contractor under NFPA 2001; life-safety is never DIY. |
| Switchgear, ATS, generator, SPD | Listed equipment; selective coordination and arc-flash study rely on OEM data. |
| Sensors, MCU board, PoE switch, gateway SBC | Commodity electronics at $10–175; no value in making. |

| Make (Schreiber AI) | Why |
|---|---|
| Insulation & interior fit-out (PIR + steel liner + hat channel) | Commodity materials ($52/sheet, $32/panel); the labour is our assembly process and our thermal design. |
| Hot-aisle containment roof/riser panels | Polycarbonate + extrusion at ~$350/rack vs $26–45k MSRP for a Chatsworth BTS aisle kit; we buy only the Tripp Lite door/frame kits. |
| BEN Node carrier PCB and node assembly | $18 board + $39 MCU + sensors; the firmware, calibration and enclosure wiring are our IP. |
| Skid base for Micro | Simple weldment ($1.2k). |
| Software: gateway, twin, dashboard, commissioning, calibration | Core product. |
| Container modification design (cutouts, reinforcement) | We design; a certified welder executes ($135/h, SVC-WELDER). |

## 6. Minimum-purchased-parts reasoning

The catalog is 113 lines but a given unit draws 59–82 of them; of those, only **~20 are distinct engineered purchases** (container,
rack, PDU, UPS, battery, cooling unit, condenser/dry cooler, CDU, panelboard, main breaker, SPD, ATS/generator option, clean agent,
VESDA, EPO, PoE switch, gateway, MCU board, 4 sensor types, relay). Everything else is commodity material, site work or labour.
Decisions that kept the count down:

- One rack PDU type (AP8841) for every tier, A/B per rack; metering comes from the PDU plus one SDM630 per power path, not a
  separate branch-circuit monitor.
- One in-row DX model (ACRD301S) covers BEN 10 and BEN 20; one UPS family per voltage class (SRT for 208 V 1-ph, 93PM for 208 V 3-ph,
  Galaxy VS for 480 V) instead of per-tier models.
- Controls node is one board design for all tiers; node count scales with racks.
- Hot-aisle containment buys only the door and frame kits; panels are made.
- Containment, lighting, insulation, liner and fasteners come from big-box channels so any field team can replenish.

## 7. Known weaknesses to fix with quotes

1. **BEN Micro cooling:** `config.py` sizes 3 × 5.8 kW coolers (N+1) for 6 kW IT. Three wall coolers will not physically fit a 24U
   cabinet; the quote (email 02/03) asks for a cabinet that can carry two 6 kW-class units, or a 42U/double-bay. The Great Lakes
   cabinet's integral 2,200 BTU AC is a placeholder shell price.
2. **Battery sizing:** one 32.6 kWh Li-ion cabinet per UPS frame is coarse; BEN 40 shows 4 cabinets ($221k) for 35.6 kWh needed.
   Email 09 asks for module-level right-sizing.
3. **480→208 V transformers** (estimate $9.5k/$17k each, two per unit) exist because AP8841 PDUs are 208 V. A 415/240 V distribution
   or 480 V-input PDUs would remove them; decision deferred to the power-chain design.
4. **BEN 40 cooling is 100 % estimate** (CDU, manifolds, dry cooler, piping ≈ $390k). Emails 05–07 target this.
5. **SITE/SVC** are national-average estimates; regional bids (email 15) will replace them per site.
6. **FS for Micro:** we decided *not* to exclude FS from Micro; it gets an aerosol unit, E-stop, extinguishers and smoke detection
   instead of a piped clean-agent system (which `applies_to_tiers` restricts to container tiers). Reversible once a fire contractor
   comments (email 11).
