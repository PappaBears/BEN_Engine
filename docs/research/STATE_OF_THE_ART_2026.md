# State of the art, November 2026 — 800 VDC power and high-density liquid cooling for AI data centers

**Status:** research file for `docs/FORWARD_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION.md` and `docs/programs/FORWARD_800V_LIQUID_PROGRAM.md`. Compiled 2026-11-09. Every URL used is listed with access date in `docs/research/SOURCES.md`.

**How to read this file.** Plain language. Every figure carries a link and a date. "Vendor claim" means the number comes from the company that sells the product and has not been measured by anyone independent. "Not found" means we searched and could not source it; it is not a statement that the thing does not exist. Where sources disagree, both numbers are shown.

**Method and limits.** Web search with quoted excerpts was the only working channel in this session; direct page fetches to nvidia.com, opencompute.org, se.com, vertiv.com, microsoft.com, datacenterdynamics.com and most other primary hosts were blocked by the session's network policy. Primary PDFs (OCP Diablo 400, OCP SST v0.3, Schneider WP219/WP220, NVIDIA 800 VDC white paper, Lenovo GB300 guide) are therefore cited from their indexed text, not read end to end. Section 6 lists the documents that must be read in full before any number is reused in a product page.

**What "DC-ready" has to mean for a 150–500 kW BEN (our synthesis of the above; not a sourced requirement list):**

1. A DC distribution that can be configured as **±400 VDC with HR midpoint** (Diablo 400 convention) or **800 VDC unipolar with isolated return** (NVIDIA/OCP SST convention), because both will reach integrators in 2027.
2. Rack interfaces at three levels: 800 VDC/±400 VDC direct to an MGX-class rack; 800 V → 50 V shelf (Delta/Flex/LiteOn/Vertiv-type) for ORv3 racks; and conventional AC PDUs, since BEN 5/30 and most enterprise IT stay AC.
3. An **AC/DC front end at 150–500 kW** that does not exist as a listed product yet: either a SiC rectifier sized like one Delta/Flex 110 kW shelf × N, or a sub-MW SST (DG Matrix is at 400 kW per module). The 2–10 MW SST class (OCP v0.3, ABB, Eaton, Siemens) is too big for a module.
4. IMD + fault location + capacitor-energy inventory as standard, because the first fault on an IT/HRG bus is silent.
5. A documented AHJ and field-evaluation path (UL 508A DC panel, NRTL field label), since no listing exists.
6. Battery on bus sized for sub-second smoothing and minutes of ride-through (the two roles NVIDIA and Envision describe), not hours.

### 2.11 What a sealed, relocatable module should do differently (our synthesis)

1. **Design the loop for W45 flow, not W27 flow.** The GB300 table (177 LPM/rack at 45 °C vs 59 at 25 °C) means pipe, pump and CDU sizing triples; the reward is dry-cooler-only heat rejection (ASHRAE W45 drops the chiller) and halving of water use (Schneider WP220).
2. **Treat leak-to-isolation as a commissioned number.** OCP gives the vocabulary (direct/indirect detection, automatic intervention by stopping flow or de-energising) but no benchmark exists; a module can time it on every FAT and publish it.
3. **Consider negative-pressure or dual-mode CDUs** (Chilldyne class) for the secondary loop in a sealed box where a spill has nowhere to go.
4. **Specify to OCP interfaces** (UQD v2, PG25 or water spec v1.3, cold-plate test spec) so rack vendors are interchangeable.
5. **Put the CDU and pumps on the DC bus** when `dc800` is selected (Delta already powers a 2.5 MW CDU from 800 VDC), so cooling rides through on the same battery as IT.
6. **Expose a heat-reuse port and account for it** (kWh thermal delivered, temperature, hours), because EU EED Art. 26 and Germany's EnEfG make heat reuse a reporting and permitting matter from 300 kW–1 MW upward.

## 5. The three biggest uncertainties

1. **Which DC convention wins at the rack: ±400 VDC bipolar with HR midpoint (Diablo 400) or 800 VDC unipolar with isolated return (NVIDIA/OCP SST v0.3).** They share a bus voltage but not grounding, insulation coordination or protection. Sources disagree on whether they converge; "Mt. Diablo 2.0" is unconfirmed. BEN must design for both until one is settled, which raises cost.
2. **Whether anything listable arrives in time.** No UL-listed 800 VDC power shelf, sidecar, busway or breaker was found; NEC coverage is pointed at 2029 with enforcement later; UL's SST program began in Sep 2026 and DG Matrix's Q2 2026 UL target has no confirmation. The program's field-evaluation path may be the only route through 2028, and some AHJs and insurers may not accept it.
3. **Real rack power and water temperature for the 2027 platforms.** Vera Rubin NVL72 has no official wattage (190–250 kW range); Kyber's 600 kW was not restated and may be ~300 kW; the 45 °C design point rests on one secondary source for DSX and on Lenovo's GB300 table. Whether a 150–500 kW module holds one rack or two, and at what flow, depends on numbers NVIDIA has not published.
