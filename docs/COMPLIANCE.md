# BEN Engine — Compliance Matrix

**Owner:** Schreiber AI compliance & operations. **Status:** working draft for AHJ pre-application meetings. **Edition years checked:** 2026-11-07 (web check of publishers' catalogues; where the publisher's own page could not be reached this is marked *verify with AHJ*).

This document is written so a plant director can read it, understand what applies to a BEN Engine unit on their site, and defend each line to their boss, their insurer and the local inspector. Compliance is a design input here, not an appendix. Every row names the specific BEN Engine feature that satisfies the requirement and how it is proven.

How to read the **Status** column:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Design feature** | Built into every unit of the stated tiers. Proven at FAT (factory acceptance test) and recorded in the turnover package. |
| **Needs listing** | A purchased component or the assembled panel must carry an NRTL listing or a certification that we must obtain or verify before shipment. |
| **Site-dependent** | Depends on the customer's site, state/county adoption, utility, or AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction). Resolved during the site survey and permit stage (see `docs/SITE_REQUIREMENTS.md`). |
| **Informative** | Voluntary guideline. We use it as a design target and say so; we do not claim certification. |

Who certifies, abbreviations: **NRTL** = OSHA Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory (UL Solutions, Intertek/ETL, CSA, TÜV); **AHJ** = the local building, electrical and fire officials; **PE** = licensed Professional Engineer stamping calculations or drawings; **Mfr** = manufacturer's declaration/test report; **C3PAO** = CMMC third-party assessment organization; **QSA** = PCI Qualified Security Assessor.

Tier abbreviations: **5** = BEN 5, **30** = BEN 30, **150** = BEN 150, **500** = BEN 500, **2000** = BEN 2000. "All" = 5/30/150/500/2000. Hardening levels H1/H2/H3 per `docs/TIER_AND_ENCLOSURE_DECISION.md` §3.

## 0. The six findings that change the design

These came out of building this matrix. Each is a reversible decision recorded in `docs/PROJECT_NOTES.md`.

| # | Finding | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A2L refrigerant charge limits make conventional in-row DX with R-454B/R-32 non-compliant inside a container.** UL 60335-2-40 (4th ed.) and ASHRAE 15 limit the refrigerant that may be released into an occupiable space to roughly 25 % of the lower flammability limit (LFL). For R-454B (LFL ≈ 0.306 kg/m³) the unmitigated allowable charge for a floor-standing unit in a BEN 150 (12.7 m² floor, 32 m³) is about **1.2 kg**, and about 2.5 kg using the simpler 25 %·LFL·volume screen. A 30 kW in-row DX unit holds 8–15 kg. BEN 30 is worse (0.8 kg / 1.2 kg). Mitigation routes (listed leak detection + forced ventilation + safety shut-off valves) exist but add cost, failure modes and AHJ friction. | **BEN 30 and BEN 150 in-row DX units are specified with A1 (non-flammable) refrigerant that also meets the AIM Act GWP < 700 limit: R-513A (GWP ≈ 630) or R-515B (GWP ≈ 300).** Where a vendor only offers A2L, the refrigerant circuit stays outside: use the `rdhx_drycooler` or a packaged chiller + chilled-water in-row option so no flammable refrigerant enters the ITE space. BEN 5 cabinet DX may use A2L only if the cabinet AC is listed to UL 60335-2-40 as a unit and the cabinet is marked "not for human occupancy" (the LFL-based limit for a 1.1 m³ cabinet is ~0.3 kg, so even here A1 is preferred). BEN 500 is liquid-cooled; the dry cooler loop is water/glycol, no refrigerant inside. |
| 2 | **AIM Act Technology Transitions rule.** EPA's 2023 rule sets a **700 GWP limit for new data center / computer-room / IT-equipment-room cooling installed on or after 1 January 2027** (date confirmed in several secondary sources; EPA's May 2026 reconsideration rule extended dates for other sectors and we found no evidence it moved the data-center date — *verify on the EPA sector table before each engineering release*). R-410A (GWP 2088) is out. | Every refrigerant-charged line in `purchasing/parts_catalog.json` (`CLG-INROW`, `CLG-COND`, `CLG-REFRIG`, `CLG-CABAC`) carries `refrigerant` and `gwp` attributes, `software/ben_engine/bom.py` prints them as a *Refrigerant / GWP* column in `docs/BOM-<tier>.md` / `purchasing/bom-<tier>.csv`, and M-001 of each DX tier carries the refrigerant and charge note; the limit is < 700. BEN 30/150 in-row DX: R-513A, GWP 631 — compliant. **BEN 5 cabinet AC (`CLG-CABAC`, Rittal Blue e+ 3189.940): the catalogued 5.8 kW rating is the R-134a variant, GWP 1430 — NOT compliant.** Rittal offers an R-513A variant (5.5 kW, GWP 631) and is moving Blue e+ to R-1234yf; the catalog line carries an RFQ note to quote the A1 < 700 GWP variant and to re-check the 5.5 kW capacity against the BEN 5 load before engineering release. Until that quote is in, BEN 5 is flagged open on this finding. | **Update (design change DC-001, drawing Rev C):** the BEN 5 unit is now specified as the R-513A 5.5 kW variant (GWP 631); the part number and price are pending Rittal's answer to RFQ 03, and the N+1 pair covers the 5 kW load with margin.
| 3 | **Lithium battery cabinets ≥ 20 kWh bring in NFPA 855 and UL 9540.** `battery_kwh` from the config is 1.1 (BEN 5), 5.6 (BEN 30), 17.8–27.8 (BEN 150), 35.6–92.6 (BEN 500) and ~300 (BEN 2000, 2N strings) at the default 10-minute ride-through. NFPA 855 applies to lithium-ion ESS above 20 kWh, including UPS batteries, and requires UL 9540-listed systems, UL 9540A test data, spacing (≤ 50 kWh per group, 0.9 m between groups unless 9540A data allows less), and explosion control for walk-in enclosures. | **BEN 5 and BEN 30 stay below 20 kWh by design** (ride-through ≤ 10 min). **BEN 150 is capped at 20 kWh total lithium unless the customer accepts the 855 package**; the selector warns above 20 kWh. **BEN 500 always carries the 855 package:** UL 9540-listed LFP cabinets with unit-level UL 9540A report showing no cell-to-cell propagation, placed in a dedicated battery compartment with its own 1-hour-rated partition, gas detection, and deflagration venting per NFPA 69 sized by the 9540A data, or in an external battery enclosure. Generator becomes the preferred long-ride-through solution, not more battery. |
| 4 | **NEC Article 645 is optional and we do not invoke it by default.** Art. 645 relaxes wiring rules under raised floors in exchange for an HVAC-shutdown disconnect, dedicated HVAC, separation, etc. BEN Engine has no raised floor, so the relaxations buy nothing. | All wiring is done to NEC Chapters 1–4 (Chapter 3 wiring methods throughout). We **still** provide the NFPA 75 items the fire marshal expects: EPO/remote disconnect at the exit door, HVAC shutdown on detection, listed power-distribution equipment. Decision reversible if an AHJ asks for 645 compliance. |
| 5 | **The container is a "structure" to most AHJs, and a walk-in BEN 30/150/500 needs a building permit.** Occupancy is usually Group U (utility, not normally occupied) or S-2/F-2; some AHJs apply B. ASCE 7 wind/seismic anchorage is required either way. BEN 5 is a cabinet on a skid and is normally treated as equipment, not a structure. | Each tier ships with a **PE-stamped anchorage and foundation design** for the climate's wind speed (47–67 m/s from `CLIMATE_DESIGN`) and site seismic category, a code summary sheet for the AHJ pre-application meeting, and an "equipment enclosure vs. building" position paper (section 6 below). |
| 6 | **NEC 110.26 working space at 480 V drives the interior layout.** Panels operating at 151–600 V need 1.07 m (42 in.) clear depth in front for Condition 2 (grounded surface opposite), 0.76 m (30 in.) width, and a 2.0 m (6.5 ft) headroom; equipment ≥ 1200 A and > 1.8 m wide needs two exits. Container internal width is 2.20 m. | Power panels mount on the end wall with a **dedicated 1.1 m electrical vestibule** inside the door on BEN 150/500; nothing may be stored there (floor is marked). BEN 500 at 1000–1200 A frames gets a second egress door at the far end, which also satisfies OSHA egress. |
