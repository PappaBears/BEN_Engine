# BEN Engine — Investor briefing: speaker notes

## 1. BEN Engine: AI infrastructure that arrives finished

Schreiber AI turns a mission profile into an all-in-one data center, power, cooling, compute and controls in one sealed box, built, tested and documented, and keeps watching it. The same system scales to a 2 MW center.

Open with the outcome, not the technology. The claim to make: a customer picks a mission profile and gets a finished, commissioned unit with a risk list they saw before the price. The claim to avoid: anything about hardware we have not built yet. Transition: why does this not exist already for small and mid-size businesses?

## 2. The problem: AI wants to live where the data is, and nobody builds small

SMBs face a cloud bill that grows with success, colocation priced for hyperscalers, or a server closet nobody commissioned.

All three numbers are cited in docs/investors/MARKET_SOURCES.md with ranges. Say the ranges out loud; investors respect ranges more than point estimates. Transition: the product family. The BEN 2000 figure comes from docs/BOM-ben2000.md and scripts/unit_economics.py (infrastructure-only sell price $16.4 M for 1,600 kW IT); say 'about $10,200 per kW infrastructure-only vs $10,000–13,000 per kW traditional' and that most of that BOM is quote-only estimates.

## 3. The product family

One engineering model. The core product is the all-in-one box, BEN 5 to BEN 500; BEN 2000 shows the same system at 2 MW.

Point out that the cooling defaults changed during the build because the interference checks in the drawing generator proved that 600 mm in-row units do not fit a 20 ft row at 96 kW. That is the engineering system working. Transition: what makes it defensible. BEN 2000 is built by modular-building manufacturers, not container shops; its twin, drawings, BOM and commissioning run in simulation (docs/VALIDATION.md section 4a) while every physical value is unverified, so present it as sized and documented, not as proven.

## 4. Element-proof and hardened

Every unit is NEMA 4X / IP66 and hose-tested; H2 and H3 are priced packages that turn a bespoke engineering project into a catalog option.

Hardening is a margin lever and a market opener (public sector, defence, utilities, coasts). Never say bulletproof, EMP-proof or TEMPEST certified: say UL 752 level, MIL-STD-188-125-1 option, TEMPEST-ready. Vendor certificates are required with every shipment (SUPPLIER_TERMS.md).

## 5. Why we win: the configuration-driven engineering system

Drawings, bill of materials, compliance pack, assembly instructions, digital twin and commissioning report all come from the same configuration.

This is the moat. A competitor can buy the same container and UPS. They cannot buy the system that makes every unit identical and testable with few engineers. Demo backup: docs/proposals/ shows a full customer proposal generated by one command. Transition: show the twin.

## 6. You can run the box before it exists

The dashboard that ships in every unit runs in simulation mode: 3-D twin, walkthrough, automated sweeps, fault injection, calibration, CFD, CSV export.

Live demo plan: bash scripts/run_dashboard.sh, open localhost:8080, change tier to BEN 500, inject a cooling failure, watch the standby unit start, run the failover sweep, download the CSV. Recovery plan if the demo fails: the screenshots in docs/media/screens and the sample commissioning report. Caveat to say out loud: the twin's thermal time constants are design values, so the two-minute number is a lower bound until measured on hardware.
