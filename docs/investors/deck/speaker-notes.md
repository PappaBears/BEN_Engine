# BEN Engine — Investor briefing: speaker notes

## 1. BEN Engine: AI infrastructure that arrives finished

Schreiber AI turns a mission profile into a built, tested, documented data center in an off-the-shelf container, and keeps watching it.

Open with the outcome, not the technology. The claim to make: a customer picks a mission profile and gets a finished, commissioned unit with a risk list they saw before the price. The claim to avoid: anything about hardware we have not built yet. Transition: why does this not exist already for small and mid-size businesses?

## 2. The problem: AI wants to live where the data is, and nobody builds small

SMBs face a cloud bill that grows with success, colocation priced for hyperscalers, or a server closet nobody commissioned.

All three numbers are cited in docs/investors/MARKET_SOURCES.md with ranges. Say the ranges out loud; investors respect ranges more than point estimates. Transition: the product family.

## 3. The product family

One engineering model, four enclosures, every subsystem selectable.

Point out that the cooling defaults changed during the build because the interference checks in the drawing generator proved that 600 mm in-row units do not fit a 20 ft row at 96 kW. That is the engineering system working. Transition: what makes it defensible.

## 4. Why we win: the configuration-driven engineering system

Drawings, bill of materials, compliance pack, assembly instructions, digital twin and commissioning report all come from the same configuration.

This is the moat. A competitor can buy the same container and UPS. They cannot buy the system that makes every unit identical and testable with few engineers. Demo backup: docs/proposals/ shows a full customer proposal generated by one command. Transition: show the twin.

## 5. You can run the box before it exists

The dashboard that ships in every unit runs in simulation mode: 3-D twin, walkthrough, automated sweeps, fault injection, calibration, CFD, CSV export.

Live demo plan: bash scripts/run_dashboard.sh, open localhost:8080, change tier to BEN 500, inject a cooling failure, watch the standby unit start, run the failover sweep, download the CSV. Recovery plan if the demo fails: the screenshots in docs/media/screens and the sample commissioning report. Caveat to say out loud: the twin's thermal time constants are design values, so the two-minute number is a lower bound until measured on hardware.

## 6. Compliance is the product

The compliance pack is generated per mission profile; it already changed the design three times.

Investors in this space have been burned by permitting. Lead with the three findings that changed the design; they prove the matrix is not decoration. Edition years are listed in docs/COMPLIANCE.md with what could not be verified.

## 7. Business model and unit economics

Prices are built up from a real bill of materials with a confidence split, not top-down.

The exact table is regenerated by scripts/unit_economics.py into VC_PROPOSITION.md. Be explicit that 'confirmed' in this session means a distributor listing seen in search results on 2026-11-07, not a quote; the 15 RFQ emails queued to Ben@SchreiberAI.com convert those into quotes. Compute dominates the all-modules price; most SMB customers will start infrastructure-only.

## 8. Asset-light manufacturing: third parties first

Contract manufacturers build to our drawings and instructions; integrators sell and install; Schreiber AI owns design, controls, commissioning and operations.

This is where the money does not go: no building. See docs/MANUFACTURING_STRATEGY.md for the CM selection scoring and the transition thresholds. Caveat: no CM has been qualified yet; the RFI is queued.

## 9. Go-to-market

Lighthouse units first, then integrators as the channel, with profile-led marketing that discloses risks before price.

The product page is in web/ and renders without a backend; the proposal generator is the back end behind the slider. Transition: what the money is for.
